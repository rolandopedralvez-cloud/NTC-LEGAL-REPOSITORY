Memorandum Circular No. 10-07-2007 — Mandating the Development of Reference Access Offers (RAO) to Facilitate Fair and Expeditious Interconnection or Access Between Service Providers

Status
Active
Issued
July 19, 2007
Legal Basis
RA 7925; Commonwealth Act 146 (Public Service Law), Sec. 19(b); EO 59
Issued By
Commissioner Abraham R. Abesamis; Deputy Commissioners Jorge V. Sarmiento, Jaime M. Fortes Jr.

Companion circular to MC 09-07-2007 (LEC Interconnection), issued because the Commission found that market forces alone couldn't ensure non-dominant players secure fair interconnection in a market dominated by a few large players.

Article I — Definitions

Defines Access, Access Provider, Access Seeker, Interconnection, and Reference Access Offer (RAO) — a default offer/agreement containing terms and conditions, including prices, on which a PTE is prepared to provide access to any access seeker.

Article II — Reference Access Offers (RAO)

All authorized PTEs must submit an RAO to the Commission for each applicable access service: fixed/mobile network origination-transit-termination, fixed/mobile internet access call services, retail narrowband/broadband (for VASP connection), and mobile data services. RAOs must be comprehensive enough that an access seeker can accept without negotiation, covering points of interconnection, network requirements, charging mechanisms, technical commitments, confidentiality, and dispute resolution. RAO terms are valid for 3 years (or as short as 1 year for price terms, with clearly stated modification triggers).

Access seekers may accept the RAO as-is (access provider must execute within 30 days of acceptance notice) or negotiate an individualized agreement instead — both paths require Commission submission within 10 days of execution, with 30-day deemed-approval if the Commission doesn't act. The Commission publishes RAO Development Guidelines within 60 days of this Circular's effectivity; access providers then have 60 days to submit their RAOs and cost models.

Articles III–IV — Transitory Provisions, Penalties

Existing interconnection agreements get 1 year to renegotiate into compliance. Non-compliance carries the same penalties as EO 59 violations: administrative fines/sanctions and/or suspension of pending/future permit applications, after due notice and hearing.

Article V — Final Provisions

Invalid provisions don't affect the rest of the Circular. Inconsistent prior issuances repealed/amended. Effective 15 days after publication, with 3 certified copies to the UP Law Center. Quezon City, July 19, 2007.
