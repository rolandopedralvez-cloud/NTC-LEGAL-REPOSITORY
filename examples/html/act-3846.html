Foundational Laws

Act No. 3846 — An Act Providing for the Regulation of Public and Radio Communications in the Philippines and for Other Purposes (Radio Control Law)

Status
Amended, still in force — the foundational law cited by nearly every other document in this repository
Approved
November 11, 1931
Amended By
Com. Act 365, Com. Act 571 (1940); Act 584 (1950)

Section 1 — Franchise Requirement

No person, firm, company, association, or corporation may construct, install, establish, or operate a radio transmitting station, a radio receiving station used for commercial purposes, or a radio broadcasting station without first obtaining a franchise from the Congress of the Philippines. Exceptions: amateur stations, experimental stations, training stations, stations aboard mobile vessels/trains/aircraft, and private stations in outlying/remote areas without access to a public communication system. Broadcasting stations already operating at the time of the Act's approval were exempt from the franchise requirement. As amended by Com. Act 365 and Com. Act 571 (approved June 17, 1940).

Section 1-A — Registration

No person, firm, company, association, or corporation may possess or own transmitters or transceivers without registering them with the Secretary of Public Works and Communications, nor sell/transfer them without prior approval; construction, manufacture, or purchase of radio transmitters/transceivers requires a permit from the Secretary. As amended by Act 584 (approved September 18, 1950).

Section 2 — Construction Permit and License

Station construction/installation requires a permit from the Secretary of Commerce and Industry; operation requires a license stating the valid operating dates. Renewal applications must be submitted at least 2 months before expiry. Maximum license period: 3 years.

Section 3 — Regulatory Powers

Empowers the Secretary of Public Works and Communications to regulate construction, manufacture, possession, control, sale, and transfer of radio transmitters/transceivers, and the establishment, use, and operation of all radio stations and communications. Specific powers include: prescribing construction/manufacture/possession/sale rules; classifying stations and prescribing services per class; assigning call letters and frequencies; promulgating interference-prevention rules (with a hearing required before changing an existing station's frequency, power, signal type, or operating hours); establishing service areas/zones; regulating chain broadcasting; requiring record-keeping (traffic, distress watches, programs); conducting investigations, hearings, and compelling document production; regulating radio training schools; prescribing government fee rates (inspection, licensing, examination, renewal); approving/disapproving station applications and renewals (with hearing rights); pursuing criminal actions, confiscating apparatus, or suspending/revoking licenses for violations; approving station location, power, and apparatus type; and prescribing SOS/distress traffic rules consistent with International Radio Regulations. As amended by Act 584 (1950).

Section 4 — Ownership Restrictions

License transfers require express Secretary approval. Licenses may not be granted or transferred to non-citizens of the Philippines or the United States (as applicable at time of enactment); to firms/companies not incorporated under Philippine or U.S. state/territory law; or to any company where an alien serves as officer/director, or where 20%+ of voting capital stock is held by aliens, foreign governments, or foreign-organized entities.

Later Legal Interpretation — Franchise Requirement Superseded by Administrative Authority

A 1991 Department of Justice opinion (responding to an NTC query) concluded that EO 546 — by integrating the former Board of Communications and Telecommunications Control Bureau into NTC and granting it broad certificate/permit-issuing authority under its Section 15 — authorizes NTC to issue authorizations for radio/TV broadcasting operation without requiring a prior legislative franchise under Section 1 of this Act, notwithstanding the franchise requirement as originally written. This interpretation traces through PD 576-A (1974), which terminated all existing broadcast franchises effective December 31, 1981, and vested authorization power in the (then) Board of Communications and Secretary of Public Works and Communications, predecessors to NTC's current authority.
