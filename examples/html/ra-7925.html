Republic Act No. 7925 — Public Telecommunications Policy Act of the Philippines

Status
Active
Approved
March 1, 1995
Type
Republic Act

Article I — General Provisions

Section 1. Short Title. This Act shall be known as the "Public Telecommunications Policy Act of the Philippines."

Section 2. Scope and Application. This Act shall apply to all public telecommunications entities in the Philippines.

Section 3. Definitions. Key terms defined in this Act include telecommunications, public telecommunications entity, broadcasting, franchise, local exchange operator, inter-exchange carrier, international carrier, value-added service provider (VAS), public toll calling station, mobile radio telephone system, and interconnection.

Article II — Policy and Objectives

Section 4. Declaration of National Policy. Telecommunications is declared essential to national economic development, integrity, and security. The Act sets national policy priorities including universal service infrastructure, expansion to unserved areas, fair spectrum allocation, reasonable rates, private-sector-led growth, healthy competition, interconnection, international carrier support, administrative due process, broad ownership, domestic manufacturing, and workforce development.

Article III — Administration

Section 5. Responsibilities of the National Telecommunications Commission. The Commission is designated principal administrator of this Act, responsible for facilitating market entry, ensuring service quality and interoperability, mandating fair interconnection, promoting consumer welfare, and protecting against monopolistic abuse. See EO 546 (creating the Commission).

Section 6. Responsibilities of and Limitations to Department Powers. The Department of Transportation and Communications is barred from influencing the Commission's quasi-judicial functions, but coordinates on national development planning, R&D, international representation, and industry consultation.

Article IV — Telecommunications Entities

Sections 7–13 define categories of telecommunications entities and their obligations: local exchange operators (Sec. 8), inter-exchange carriers (Sec. 9), international carriers (Sec. 10), value-added service providers (Sec. 11), mobile radio services (Sec. 12), and radio paging services (Sec. 13).

Article V — Other Services and Facilities

Section 14. Customer Premises Equipment. Subscribers may use type-approved terminal equipment on their premises.

Section 15. Radio Frequency Spectrum. Spectrum allocation is subject to periodic review, user fees, and open tenders where demand exceeds availability.

Article VI — Franchise, Rates and Revenue Determination

Section 16. Franchise. No entity may conduct public telecommunications business without a franchise; the Commission issues Certificates of Public Convenience and Necessity (CPCN) of 5 years to the life of the franchise.

Section 17. Rates and Tariffs. Rates must be fair and reasonable; the Commission retains residual power to set floors/ceilings if competition fails.

Section 18. Access Charge/Revenue Sharing. Interconnection revenue-sharing is negotiated between carriers, with Commission arbitration on dispute and cross-subsidy obligations for unprofitable local exchange areas.

Section 19. Uniform System of Accounts. Telecom entities must maintain uniform, category-separated accounts.

Article VII — Rights of Telecommunications Users

Section 20. End users are entitled to non-discriminatory reliable service, timely connection, accurate billing, and prompt complaint investigation.

Article VIII — Telecommunications Development

Section 21. Public Ownership. Regulated telecom entities must publicly offer at least 30% of aggregate common stock within 5 years.

Section 22. Privatization of Existing Facilities. Government-owned telecom facilities must be privatized within 3 years, generally via public bidding.

Section 23. Equality of Treatment. Favorable terms granted to any franchise automatically extend to all existing franchises (most-favored-franchisee clause).

Article IX — Final Provisions

Section 24. Transitory Provision. Deregulated services may keep Commission-approved rates through the year of effectivity; existing but inactive franchises are deemed revoked.

Section 25. Separability Clause. Invalid provisions do not nullify the remainder of the Act.

Section 26. Repealing Clause. All inconsistent laws, ordinances, rules, and regulations are repealed or modified accordingly.

Section 27. Effectivity Clause. Effective 15 days after publication in at least two newspapers of general circulation.

Approved: March 1, 1995.
