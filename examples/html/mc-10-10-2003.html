Memorandum Circular No. 10-10-2003 — Implementing Rules and Regulations Governing Community Antenna/Cable Television (CATV) and Direct Broadcast Satellite (DBS) Services to Promote Competition in the Sector

Status
Active — subject to a 10-year sunset clause on its exclusivity provisions (see Article 8)
Issued
October 4, 2003
Legal Basis
Department Circular No. 2003-01 (March 3, 2003); Art. XII, Sec. 19 of the Philippine Constitution
Issued By
Commissioner Armi Jane R. Borje; Deputy Commissioners Kathleen G. Heceta, Jorge V. Sarmiento

Issued to promote fair competition and prevent monopolistic practices in the CATV/DBS (direct-to-home) television market, consistent with the constitutional mandate against monopolies and unfair trade combinations.

Article 1 — Definition of Terms

CATV/Community Antenna Television: a facility that receives, amplifies/modifies, and distributes broadcast signals by wire/fiber/cable to paying subscribers. DBS: satellite dish-delivered TV service directly to homes (direct-to-home/direct-to-user). Exclusive Contracts: arrangements granting sole broadcast rights to one/some CATV/DBS operators, excluding others. Program/Content Provider: any entity distributing/selling TV programs and signals to authorized operators.

Article 2 — Development of Competition and Diversity

General rule: exclusive contracts between CATV/DBS operators and program/content providers are presumed anti-competitive and prohibited. Exceptions require a Commission petition, granted only if all parties agree to Philippine jurisdiction for disputes and all content providers (foreign providers via a local agent) are registered with the Commission. Petitions require published notice 15 days before hearing; the Commission decides within 30 days of initial hearing.

Article 3 — Existing Contracts

Existing contracts are presumed valid, but the Commission may review any contract motu proprio or upon petition to determine if it establishes prohibited exclusivity; unjustified exclusivity clauses are voided without affecting the rest of the contract.

Article 4 — Criteria for Determining Approval

Considers: effect on competition/small-new-entrant protection, effect on investment (especially unserved/underserved areas), effect on programming diversity, contract duration, and other factors relevant to healthy competition and local content development.

Articles 5–6 — Confidentiality, Operator Obligations

Reviewed contracts are protected as confidential. Operators may not receive/distribute/transmit TV signals without a program/content provider agreement, and must observe self-censorship policy in program retransmission.

Articles 7–8 — Penalties, Sunset Provision

Violations subject to administrative fines/penalties/sanctions after due notice and hearing, including possible suspension/revocation of authorizations. The exclusivity prohibition automatically expires 10 years after effectivity unless expressly extended by the Commission following public notice and consultation.

Articles 9–11 — Separability, Repeal, Effectivity

Invalid provisions don't affect the rest of the Circular. Inconsistent prior issuances are repealed/amended. Effective 15 days after publication in the Official Gazette or a newspaper of general circulation, with 3 certified copies to the UP Law Center.
